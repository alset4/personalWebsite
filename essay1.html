On Zuck

On Mark Zuckerberg. Just last night I watched the podcast episode of Theo Von's 'Last Weekend Podcast' where his guest was Mark Zuckerberg.
I personally enjoyed episode though I did not realize how corny Mark really is, granted he is a out-of-touch tech billionaire, so corny is probably a given.
Nonetheless, I found the episode pretty harmless, but to my suprise, all of Reddit rallied against the episode and against Theo, maybe even moreso than when he had Trump on it a few months earlier.
And overall, there seems to a pervading hatred of Mark on the internet that I myself was not aware of, but the dislikes of redditors usually does not draw me in too far, but the reason behind the Zuckerberg hate was especially interesting to me and reflects the idea of free will in society as a whole.
People don't really hate on Zuck for his stealing of Facebook from the Vinklevoss twins, or his stealing of data, but moreso for his platform making society a worse place, ensuing that Meta's subsidiaries are all addictive platforms that reel in users unknowingly.

I'm personally not a fan of this argument, video games like Fortnite are currently being sued for the same reason, but the reason that I do not like this argument is because it rattles against the idea of free will.
objectively, no one is forced to use Facebook, and it is not as addictive as something that directly plays with your dopamine receptors like an amphetamin (albeit though pyschologically addictive). I think to expect an internet without platforms that use psychology and short form content to optimize for user engagement is ridicolous in itself, considering all of the Meta subsidiaries are 'entertainment platforms'.
